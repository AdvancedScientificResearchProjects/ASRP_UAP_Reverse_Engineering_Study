*Reading note, 20 Nov 2026. Up: [references](../README.md). Record: `references/sources.json`, id `iaea2015`; graph node `ref-iaea-2015-nuclear-forensics-support`.*

**Source.** International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA). Nuclear Forensics in Support of Investigations: Implementing Guide (IAEA Nuclear Security Series No. 2-G, Rev. 1). IAEA Nuclear Security Series No. 2-G (Rev. 1).
Official address: <https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1687web-74206224.pdf>.

**What was read.** full text; paragraphs around 3.11 read (PDF pages 25-26).

**Design and sample.** Implementing guide of the International Atomic Energy Agency for forensic examination of nuclear and other radioactive material. Not a study.

## What the source says

- "[The laboratory] undertaking the nuclear forensic analysis should operate under a quality assurance plan, which includes the sample chain of custody, validated analytical procedures, staff with demonstrated competencies, documented procedures, standard reporting forms and records management." (Section 3, PDF p. 25).
- "Forensic samples should be transported to laboratories using methods that maintain the chain of custody (e.g. the use of sealing devices or tags)." (para. 3.11, PDF p. 26).
- Procedures "should include statements on the control of contamination or cross-contamination" (PDF p. 25).
