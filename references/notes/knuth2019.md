# knuth2019 — Knuth (2019)

*Reading note, 20 Nov 2026. Up: [references](../README.md). Record: `references/sources.json`, id `knuth2019`; graph node `ref-knuth-2019-estimating-flight-characteristics`.*

**Source.** Knuth, Kevin H.; Powell, Robert M.; Reali, Peter A.. Estimating Flight Characteristics of Anomalous Unidentified Aerial Vehicles. Entropy 21(10) : 939.
DOI [10.3390/e21100939](https://doi.org/10.3390/e21100939).

**What was read.** full text (Europe PMC open XML, PMC7514271).

**Design and sample.** Re-analysis of published witness statements and radar accounts of a few encounters (chiefly the 2004 Nimitz case). No instrument data recorded by the authors; uncertainties are propagated from assumed error bars on the witness figures.

## What the source says

- "Estimated accelerations range from almost 100 g to 1000s of gs with no observed air disturbance, no sonic booms, and no evidence of excessive heat" (abstract).
- "these observations are either fabricated or seriously in error, or that these craft exhibit technology far more advanced than any known craft on Earth." (abstract).
- "the most probable acceleration of 5370 (+1430, −820) g" (figure caption, Nimitz radar scenario).
- A 1954 report's speed is called "Mach 55, which is comparable to the maximum speed of ∼Mach 60 we estimated in Section 2.4.1 from the radar observations".

## Verdict

**matches**

The numbers agree. Keep the paper's own caveat next to them: the inputs are testimony (a radar operator's recollection of altitudes and times), not recorded radar data, and the paper itself lists 'fabricated or seriously in error' as an alternative.
