# powell2022 — Powell (2022)

*Reading note, 20 Nov 2026. Up: [references](../README.md). Record: `references/sources.json`, id `powell2022`; graph node `ref-powell-2022-isotope-ratios-chemical`.*

**Source.** Powell, Robert M.; Swords, Michael; Rodeghier, Mark; Budinger, Phyllis. Isotope Ratios and Chemical Analysis of the 1957 Brazilian Ubatuba Fragment. Journal of Scientific Exploration 36(1) : 39-48.
DOI [10.31275/20222415](https://doi.org/10.31275/20222415).

**What was read.** full text (journal PDF, 10 pages).

**Design and sample.** New tests of one sample of the 1957 Ubatuba material by two commercial laboratories (Austin, Texas and Cleveland, Ohio) in 2017 and 2018 using high-resolution ICP-MS, with a review of the tests of the 1960s to 1980s. One sample; no blind design.

## What the source says

- Published in "Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 36, No. 1 – Spring 2022", pp. 39–48, DOI 10.31275/20222415; fourth author as printed: Phyllis Budinger.
- "The magnesium isotope ratios were found to fall within terrestrial limits while the results on the isotope ratios of the trace elements were inconclusive." (abstract, p. 39).
- A sample should "have acceptable provenance and adequate data collection" and "the Ubatuba samples are not conclusively tied to a UFO event" (p. 39).
- "One of the primary challenges is identifying two labs with sufficient experience in measuring specific isotopes so that duplicate results can be verified ... neither were experts in the specific isotopes being measured." (p. 47).
- On the two laboratories: "There is no consistent variation between the two labs as compared to the nominal terrestrial isotope abundance" (p. 46).

## Verdict

**misreported**

The card's reading of the result is right and cautious. Its venue is wrong: this is a journal article in the Journal of Scientific Exploration with a DOI, not an SCU/Zenodo report. (That journal is published by a society and is not a mainstream venue; say so, but cite it correctly.) For the campaign this is the most useful single source: it shows what goes wrong when two laboratories without isotope-specific experience measure one sample without agreed standards.
